## The Simplest Explanation

2025 papers stopped asking only about Layer 1 from the start state. Now they hand you a planning graph **mid-flight** — layers `k` and `k+1` already populated with facts, actions, and mutex pairs — and test whether you understand the *machinery*:

1. **Can layer k be the start state?**
2. **Which actions become applicable in layer k+1?**
3. **Which pairs are mutex in layer k+1 — and WHY?**

<Callout type="tip" title="Remember This">
A real state can never contain two mutex facts (they'd have to be simultaneously true and provably incompatible). And mutexes propagate forward through ALL producer pairs — including no-ops.
</Callout>

## Question 1 — Can layer k be the start state?

**Rule**: $P_0$ (start layer) is a *single reachable state* — every fact in it holds simultaneously. By definition of mutex ("can never co-occur"), **any layer containing even one mutex pair cannot be a genuine state**, so it cannot be $P_0$.

Worked check (25T3 FN Q7.1): layer k contains mutex pairs `(on(A,B), holding(A))`, `(armEmpty, clear(B))`, … Since e.g. a block can't be both on A and held by the arm, these facts can't co-exist in one state → layer k is NOT the start state. Answer: **No**.

## Question 2 — Applicable actions in layer k+1

An action appears in $A_{k+1}$ iff **all its preconditions are present AND pairwise non-mutex in layer k**.

Blocks World checks (given layer-k facts):

| Action | Preconditions present? | Any mutex among them? | In $A_{k+1}$? |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|---------------|
| Stack(A, B) | holding(A) ✓, clear(B) ✓ | holding(A) ⊕ clear(B)? not mutex here ✓ | Yes |
| Unstack(x,y) | needs on(x,y) ∧ clear(x) ∧ armEmpty | on(A,B) ⊕ holding(A) irrelevant; check actual combo | depends |
| Pickup(C) | needs onTable(C) — absent from layer k | — | **No** (fact missing beats everything) |

Two independent rejection reasons: fact simply absent, or facts present-but-mutex.

## Question 3 — Mutex propagation into layer k+1

Propositions p, q are mutex in $P_{k+1}$ iff **every** pair of their producers is mutex in $A_{k+1}$. Producers include the **no-ops**. Three sources of producer-pair mutex:

| Source | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **nop–nop inheritance** | if p ⊕ q mutex at layer k, then nop(p) ⊕ nop(q) mutex at $A_{k+1}$ |
| **nop–action interference** | action deletes p, while nop(p) persists p → they conflict |
| **action–action** | inconsistent effects / interference / competing needs |

### Why would two facts be NON-mutex in k+1?

Exhibit **one** surviving producer pair. Classic justification (25T3 Q7.4): `armEmpty` and `clear(B)` are non-mutex in layer k+1 because

- nop(armEmpty) exists,
- some action adding clear(B) (e.g., Unstack(D,A) adds Clear(A)-type facts… specifically any adder whose preconditions don't clash with ArmEmpty),
- and that action–nop pair shares no deleted/precondition conflict → not mutex → the pair escapes mutex.

One clean producer pair breaks the ALL quantifier.

### Conversely — why HOLDING(A) ⊕ ARMEMPTY stays mutex in k+1

Every producer pair is conflicted:
- nop(holding(A)) vs nop(armEmpty): inherited mutex from layer k ✓
- Stack(A,B) (deletes holding(A), adds armEmpty) vs either nop: interference/inconsistent-effects ✓
- Any other action pair touching them likewise.

ALL pairs fail → mutex. (The AN paper made this a multi-select: *all three* justification types were correct.)

## Worked Mini-Trace

Layer k facts: `{on(A,B), holding(A), clear(B), clear(D), on(D,C), armEmpty}` with known mutexes `(on(A,B),holding(A))`, `(armEmpty,clear(B))`.

1. Start-state check: mutexes exist → not $P_0$.
2. $A_{k+1}$ candidates: Stack(A,B) needs holding(A)+clear(B) — but those are **mutex** at layer k! → excluded by competing needs, despite both being individually present. Putdown(A) needs only holding(A) ✓ included. Unstack(D,A)? needs on(D,A) — absent → excluded.
3. Propagation: `on(A,B)` and `clear(B)` stay mutex at k+1 via nop–nop inheritance (their layer-k mutex propagates).

That triple rhythm — *state-check → applicability-with-mutex → propagation* — solves every subquestion.

<Accordion title="Common Exam Pitfalls">
1. **Forgetting nops when counting producers**: every fact always has its nop producer. Skipping nop–nop inheritance misses the most common mutex source.
2. **Confusing 'fact present' with 'action applicable'**: applicability also fails on mutex preconditions (competing needs) and on absent facts. Check all three.
3. **Claiming non-mutex without exhibiting a producer pair**: the exam asks WHY — cite the specific surviving pair (usually nop + benign adder).
4. **Assuming mutexes can appear spontaneously**: p,q non-mutex at k means their nops stay non-mutex forever — mutex sets only shrink across layers (monotonicity).
</Accordion>
