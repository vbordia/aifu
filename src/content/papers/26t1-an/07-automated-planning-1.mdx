| 7.1 | Consider actions a and b and the two orderings (a then b) and (b then a). Which of the following conditions (each taken independently) will make both of the ... | **A**, **B** |
| 7.2 | In planning graphs constructed by GraphPlan, actions a and b in layer n are mutex __________ . | **A**, **B** |

## The Topic in 60 Seconds

| Concept | Test |
|---|---|
| Interchangeable orderings | neither action deletes a precondition or effect of the other, and their preconditions are jointly satisfiable |
| Action mutex (layer $n$) | **inconsistent effects**: some add of one is deleted by the other; OR **interference**: one deletes a precondition/effect of the other; OR **competing needs**: some $p \in \text{pre}(a)$, $q \in \text{pre}(b)$ are proposition-mutex in layer $n{-}1$ |
| Non-mutex monotonicity | once two propositions co-exist in one layer, they co-exist in every later layer |

## Solutions

### 7.1 — Both orderings feasible → **A, B**

$(a \text{ then } b)$ and $(b \text{ then } a)$ succeed identically exactly when the actions do not step on each other:

1. **No interference**: $a$ deletes nothing that $b$ needs as precondition or brings as effect — and symmetrically for $b$ against $a$.
2. **Jointly satisfiable preconditions**: running second leaves enough of the state intact for the other's PRE list.

Any condition asserting "a must delete something of b" or demanding exclusive resources kills one of the two orders → wrong by construction.

### 7.2 — Action mutex definition → **A, B**

| Option | Reading | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | $P \in \text{pre}(a)$, $Q \in \text{pre}(b)$ are mutex in layer $n{-}1$ | **Correct** — textbook *competing needs* |
| **B** | $P$ is in $\text{pre}(a)$, in $\text{del}(a)$, in $\text{pre}(b)$ and in $\text{del}(b)$ | **Correct** — each action needs what both destroy (*inconsistent supports*); they cannot co-exist |
| C | only if EVERY precondition of $a$ is deleted by $b$ | Far too strong — ONE overlapping deletion suffices ("only if" poisons it) |
| D | only if every add-effect of $a$ is deleted by $b$ | Same disease |

### 7.3 — Non-mutex monotonicity → **A, B**

GraphPlan's persistence semantics: if $P$ and $Q$ genuinely hold together in some layer (non-mutex), the no-op edges carry both forward forever — co-existence never regresses:

- **A ✓**: non-mutex at layer 0 (the initial state) persists through all layers.
- **B ✓**: non-mutex at any layer $n$ persists to all future layers.
- **C ✗**: claims future mutex-ification — contradicts monotonicity; mutex sets can SHRINK going up, never the non-mutex relation.

The asymmetry to memorise: *mutex-ness is proved fresh each layer (may disappear); non-mutex-ness, once established, is forever.*

## Tricks & Tips

<Accordion title="Exam shortcuts">
1. 'Only if' options in definitions questions are almost always traps - GraphPlan's mutex tests are existential (ONE bad overlap), not universal.
2. Three mutex causes, three phrases: inconsistent effects, interference (deleting preconditions/effects), competing needs. Match options verbatim.
3. For ordering questions ask the single question: 'does either action delete anything the other reads or writes?' No -> interchangeable.
4. Monotonicity slogan for 7.3: mutex may vanish upward; non-mutex never does. Any option flipping this direction is wrong.
</Accordion>
