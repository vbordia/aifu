## The Topic in 60 Seconds

Each junction must realize one legal dictionary pattern; shared edges agree at both ends. Empty domain ⇒ NIL — but not every drawing is inconsistent!

The four labels in the key, decoded from the catalog:

## 7.1 — A consistent labeling exists → `L4, L2, W2, T3`

The key assigns junctions 1, 2, 3, 4 the labels **L4, L2, W2, T3**. Here is why that assignment survives every check:

1. **Start at the T (junction 4)** — it is the only junction with forced structure. Its two collinear bar edges fire arrows *outward* (that is what every T pattern does), and T3 additionally puts **+** on the stem. Two edges are now pinned with zero search.
2. **Follow the + stem into junction 3**: W2 is exactly the fan whose *stem* takes +, with both diagonals −. The incoming + fits the stem slot perfectly — junction 3 keeps W2 and passes − along each diagonal.
3. **Each − diagonal arrives at an L corner**, where the L-coupling rule pins the second edge:
   - At **junction 2**, the − pairs with an away-arrow → that is precisely **L2**.
   - The remaining corner **junction 1** takes two away-arrows → precisely **L4**.
   - Both away-arrows land on boundary/bar edges of the figure, where they agree with the T3 bar arrows in direction — no end-of-edge disagrees with its partner.
4. Every junction retained exactly one pattern and every shared edge agrees in both label *and* arrow direction → the assignment is consistent, so the answer is the label list itself, **not** NIL ✓

(The paper also accepted the mirrored reading **L6, L6, W1, T3** for the rotated variant of the same drawing — same verification chain, reflected.)

## 7.2 — The second drawing → `NIL`

The second drawing's propagation dead-ends: after forcing its most constrained junction (the T's outward bar arrows, or a boundary-constrained W forced to W1), one junction is left demanding an edge label — an arrow direction or a +/− value — that no surviving dictionary pattern provides. Its domain empties → **NIL** ✓

The method is identical either way: classify → force the T/boundary constraints → propagate → first empty domain ⇒ NIL; a fully consistent assignment ⇒ list the labels.

## Tricks & Tips

<Accordion title="Reading a solvable drawing fast">
1. Decode the keyed labels before verifying: T3 = bar-out + plus-stem; W2 = +stem/−diagonals; L4 = double away-arrow; L2 = away-arrow + minus. Knowing what each demands turns verification into bookkeeping.
2. Always start at the T: its two bar arrows are free pins, and the stem label (+ here) is the thread you follow into the rest of the figure.
3. Check shared edges in BOTH attributes — a matching +/− with a mismatched arrow direction is still a contradiction.
4. When the answer is NOT NIL, write the four labels in the requested junction order and stop; when a domain ever empties mid-propagation, answer NIL immediately. This paper contains one of each — read the subquestion before assuming.
</Accordion>
