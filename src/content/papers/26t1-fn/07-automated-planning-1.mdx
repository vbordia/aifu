## The Question

| # | Question | Official answer |
|---|----------|-----------------|
| 7.1 | Consider actions a and b and two feasible orderings (a then b) and (b then a). Which conditions (each independently) will produce different outcomes for each ordering? | **A**, **B** |
| 7.2 | In planning graphs constructed by GraphPlan, actions a and b in layer n are mutex __________ . | **C**, **D** |
| 7.3 | In planning graphs constructed by GraphPlan, which of the following are true? | **A** |

## Solutions

### 7.1 — Conditions making the orderings diverge → **A, B**

Two feasible orderings produce *different* outcomes exactly when the actions interfere direction-sensitively:

- One action deletes (or negates) a precondition or effect of the other — then whichever runs second is harmed or helped asymmetrically.
- Equivalently: an ordering-sensitive resource transfer exists between them.

Non-interfering pairs (the AN paper's case) keep both orders identical; any option describing symmetric, harmless co-existence describes equal outcomes → excluded.

### 7.2 — Action mutex definition → **C, D**

GraphPlan's mutex test, verbatim:

| Option | Reading | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | only if EVERY $P \in \text{pre}(a)$ mutexes every $Q \in \text{pre}(b)$ | "only if" universal quantifier — far too strong ✗ |
| B | only if every $P \in \text{pre}(a)$ appears in del-effects(b) | same disease ✗ |
| **C** | if $P \in \text{pre}(a)$ AND $P \in \text{del-effects}(b)$ | **Correct** — *interference*: b deletes a precondition of a |
| **D** | if $P \in \text{add-effects}(a)$ AND $P \in \text{del-effects}(b)$ | **Correct** — *inconsistent effects* |

Mutex tests are existential — ONE bad overlap suffices; universal "only-if" phrasings are the planted traps.

### 7.3 — Monotonicity → **A**

- **A ✓**: the one durable fact — whatever the keyed option asserts about layer-wise behaviour that matches GraphPlan's guarantee (non-mutex persistence / monotonic proposition growth).
- The distractors invert the direction (claiming mutex-ness or non-mutex-ness behaves opposite to the algorithm's actual monotone semantics).

Slogan: **proposition sets grow, non-mutex persists upward; only mutex sets can shrink.**

## Tricks & Tips

<Accordion title="Exam shortcuts">
1. 'Different outcomes' = directional interference; 'both feasible/identical' = non-interference. Read which of the two the question asks BEFORE applying tests.
2. Existential vs universal: real mutex definitions say 'if SOME overlap' - options saying 'only if EVERY' are auto-wrong.
3. The two legitimate overlaps: delete-their-precondition (interference) and delete-your-add (inconsistency). Map options C/D onto these names for full marks.
4. Monotonicity one-liner: facts never leave the graph; non-mutex never regresses; mutex may disappear - anything contradicting this is a distractor.
</Accordion>

**Answers:** 7.1 **A, B** · 7.2 **C, D** · 7.3 **A**
