## The Topic in 60 Seconds

Each junction must realize one legal dictionary pattern; shared edges agree at both ends. Any empty domain ⇒ **NIL**.

Structural rules that do the heavy lifting:

- **T-junction**: bar arrows point *away* from the T.
- **Dangling/boundary edges** must be occluding **arrows**.
- **W1** is the only W with arrow diagonals (pointing *into* the W); **W2/W3** carry only +/−.
- **Y patterns**: arrows-into (Y1), all − (Y2), all + (Y3) — never arrows pointing away.

## 7.1 — The L-block → `NIL`

Junctions (from the figure): **5 = L** (edges to 6, 4), **6 = L** (5, 3), **2 = L** (3, 1), **1 = L** (2, 4), **4 = W** (edges to 5, 1, 3 — fan of three), **3 = W or Y** (edges to 6, 2, 4).

Propagation sketch:

1. No T and no boundary edges — the forcing must come from the W/L interplay around the loop 5–6–3–4.
2. **W(4)**: its three edges (to 5, 1, 3) must realize W1 (+ stem, arrows into 4 on the other two), W2 (−,+,−) or W3 (+,−,+). Whichever shape is chosen, the +/−/arrow assignments propagate to L(5), L(1) and W/Y(3).
3. **L(5)**: with one edge fixed by W(4), the second edge (to 6) is pinned; **L(6)** then pins edge 6–3.
4. At **junction 3** the pinned edge 6–3 combined with the W(4)-forced edge 4–3 leaves no legal W/Y realization — every combination demands an arrow direction or +/- pair the catalog never offers. Domain empties → **NIL** ✓

(The exam expects exactly this kill-chain: classify → force the W → propagate around the loop → wipeout. Both of this paper's drawings die the same way.)

## Tricks & Tips

<Accordion title="The 4-step Waltz exam method">
1. Classify every junction (2 edges = L; collinear pair = T; 3-edge fan = W; ~120° trio = Y).
2. Force the most constrained junction first (T bars; boundary-edge arrows forcing W1).
3. Propagate edge by edge; shared edges must agree in label AND arrow direction.
4. First empty domain ⇒ NIL. Don't enumerate — eliminate.
</Accordion>
