## 7.1 — A consistent labeling exists → `L2, Y1, L3, W3`

The official answer assigns junctions 1, 2, 3, 4 as **L2, Y1, L3, W3**:

- **Junction 1 = L2**: one arrow (away) + one concave (−).
- **Junction 2 = Y1**: arrows into the junction on two edges, − on the third.
- **Junction 3 = L3**: one concave (−) + one arrow (into).
- **Junction 4 = W3**: + on one diagonal, − on the stem, + on the other diagonal.

Verification recipe:

1. **Classify** the four junctions from the drawing (two L's, one Y, one W here).
2. **Start at the Y (junction 2)** — its Y1 shape pins two arrows and a − on its three edges.
3. **Propagate** each pinned edge to its neighbour: the arrows flowing out of the Y arrive at the L junctions, selecting L2 and L3 (the arrow + − combination), and the − edge feeds the W.
4. **W (junction 4)** closes consistently with +, −, + — every junction keeps exactly one legal pattern ⇒ consistent ⇒ answer is the label list **L2, Y1, L3, W3** ✓

## 7.2 — The second drawing → `NIL`

Same method: classify, force the most constrained junction, propagate. Here the forced labels reach a junction whose remaining patterns all demand an edge label already ruled out — domain empties → **NIL** ✓

3. Propagate; strike patterns.
4. Empty domain ⇒ NIL; full consistency ⇒ list the junction labels in the requested order.
