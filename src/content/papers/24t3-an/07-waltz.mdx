## The Topic in 60 Seconds

Each junction must realize one legal dictionary pattern; shared edges must carry the same label (and arrow direction) at both ends. Empty domain ⇒ NIL — a fully consistent assignment means the answer is the label list itself.

> Deep-dive: [Waltz Line Labeling](/concepts/week-11-constraint-satisfaction/02-waltz-line-labeling).

## The dictionary entries that matter here

| Label | Geometry |
|-------|----------|
| **L2** | one arrow pointing *away* from the corner + one concave ($-$) edge |
| **L3** | one concave ($-$) edge + one arrow pointing *into* the corner |
| **Y1** | arrows *into* the junction on two edges, $-$ on the third |
| **W3** | $+$ on one diagonal, $-$ on the stem, $+$ on the other diagonal |

## 7.1 — A consistent labeling exists → `L2, Y1, L3, W3`

The official key assigns junctions 1, 2, 3, 4 = **L2, Y1, L3, W3**. How to verify it fast in the exam:

1. **Classify** the four junctions from the drawing (here: two L's, one Y, one W).
2. **Start at the Y (junction 2)** — Y1 pins arrows on two of its edges and $-$ on the third. That is the most constrained junction, so everything downstream follows from it.
3. **Propagate** each pinned edge to its neighbour: an arrow leaving the Y arrives at an L corner, selecting the arrow+$-$ pattern there (L2 if it points away from that corner, L3 if it points into it), and the $-$ edge feeds the W.
4. **Close at the W (junction 4)**: its diagonals take $+$, its stem takes $-$ — exactly W3. Every junction holds one legal pattern and every shared edge agrees at both ends ⇒ consistent ⇒ the answer is the label list **L2, Y1, L3, W3** ✓

## 7.2 — The second drawing → `NIL`

Same method: classify, force the most constrained junction, propagate. Here the forced labels reach a junction whose remaining patterns all demand an edge label already ruled out — the domain empties → **NIL** ✓

The kill typically lands like this:

1. A T-junction's bar arrows pin two edges immediately.
2. Propagation forces a neighbouring junction into a pattern that over-constrains their shared edge.
3. The last junction needs, say, two arrows pointing *into* it (any Y pattern) but every remaining edge already carries its arrowhead (or a $+/-)$ at the other end. No dictionary pattern survives → domain size zero → **inconsistent**.

## Tricks & Tips

<Accordion title="Waltz exam method, tuned to these two drawings">
1. Classify by geometry, not vibes: 2 edges = L; collinear pair + stem = T; 3-edge fan = W; ~120° trio = Y.
2. Always start at the most constrained junction — a Y with only three patterns, or any T whose bars are forced. One choice there pins 2–3 edges at once.
3. Translate each keyed label back into edge facts when verifying: L2 means "arrow leaves me"; L3 means "arrow enters me"; Y1 means "two arrows enter me". If two neighbours disagree about who the arrowhead points at, you have your NIL proof for 7.2-style drawings.
4. Never enumerate all combinations — strike patterns per junction as edges get pinned. Empty domain ends the question; full consistency means the answer is simply the label list read off in junction order.
</Accordion>
