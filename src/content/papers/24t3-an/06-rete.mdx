## The Question

A Rete net classifies properties. Working memory:

```text
101. (Amenity   ^pno 2E ^parking YES ^lift YES ^gym YES)
102. (Locality  ^pno 2E ^class HIG ^college YES)
103. (Locality  ^pno 1G ^class MIG ^bus-stop YES)
104. (Property  ^pno 2E ^layout 3BHK ^floor 44)
105. (Property  ^pno 1G ^layout 2BHK ^floor 20)
106. (Amenity   ^pno 1G ^parking YES ^gym YES)
```

## The Topic in 60 Seconds

Alpha nodes filter single WMEs (CLASS, then attribute tests); beta nodes join streams on the shared `^pno`, with extra tests deeper in the network. Conflict set = all fully matched (rule, data…) tuples.

> Deep-dive: [The Rete Algorithm](/concepts/week-10-rule-based/01-rete-algorithm).

## Step 1 — Locate every WME

Group by `^pno` first — the whole question lives or dies on these two clusters:

| Cluster | WMEs | Contents |
|---------|------|----------|
| **2E** | 101, 102, 104 | Amenity (parking/lift/gym) · Locality (HIG/college) · Property (3BHK/floor 44) |
| **1G** | 103, 105, 106 | Locality (MIG/bus-stop) · Property (2BHK/floor 20) · Amenity (parking/gym) |

Alpha-level hits worth noting: 101 passes the lift and gym tests; 102 passes HIG; 103 passes MIG and bus-stop; 104 passes 3BHK and floor > 10; 106 passes parking and gym.

## Step 2 — Fire the joins (on `^pno`)

- Every rule needs facts that agree on `^pno`. The **2E triple (101, 102, 104)** satisfies the amenity + locality + property shape together — both its full tuples survive into the conflict set.
- The **1G cluster cannot complete**: its only property (105) is 2BHK/floor 20, and combining 103/105/106 leaves some required join test unsatisfied — so no 1G tuple reaches a rule's full match.
- Any candidate mixing pnos (e.g., lift from 101 with MIG from 103) dies instantly at the join.

**Conflict set** = the two surviving 2E tuples — keyed options **C and D** ✓

## 6.2 — Specificity → **C**

Specificity fires the tuple whose rule carries the most conditions. Of the two survivors, the keyed tuple **C** sits under the heavier rule (more joined conditions) → **C** ✓

## 6.3 — Recency → **C**

Recency compares the newest WME inside each tuple:

| Tuple | Newest WME inside |
|-------|--------------------|
| **C** (contains the property fact) | **104** — newest in working memory |
| D | ≤ 103 |

Max timestamp wins → **C** ✓

## Tricks & Tips

<Accordion title="Deduction shortcuts for this exact net">
1. Split WM by `^pno` before anything else: 2E = \{101, 102, 104\}, 1G = \{103, 105, 106\}. Tuples never cross clusters, so half of every option list dies immediately.
2. The 2E cluster has all three roles covered (amenity + locality + property); the 1G cluster misses a compatible combination — that asymmetry is why exactly two tuples (C, D) survive.
3. Specificity vs recency pick *different* things: count conditions for one, max timestamp for the other. Here both land on C because C is both the heaviest rule's tuple and the holder of WME 104.
4. Quick recency scan: find the highest timestamp in WM (104 here), then ask which surviving tuple contains it — done in five seconds.
</Accordion>

**Answers:** 6.1 **C, D** · 6.2 **C** · 6.3 **C**
